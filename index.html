Why dew point, not humidity?

Warm air can hold much more water vapour than cold air, so “70% humidity” means different things at 5 °C and 25 °C. The dew point is the temperature at which the air’s water would start to condense. It measures the real amount of moisture, so LuftM compares dew points. If outdoors is at least 2 °C lower than indoors, swapping air dries the room.

Why does outdoor air feel drier once it’s inside?

Cold air that warms up keeps the same water but can hold more, so its relative humidity drops. This is a theoretical estimate; real rooms also get moisture from cooking, showers, breathing and drying clothes.

How is the heat estimate worked out?

Only for the air you swap: heat = 1.2 kg/m³ × volume × 1005 J/kg·K × temperature difference. We assume a share of the room’s air changes (you choose it), because the real amount is unknown. It is not your total heating cost.

Airing tips

A few minutes wide open beats a window tilted all day: the air changes while walls and furniture stay warm. Close up afterwards and keep heating on. Lids on pans, extractor fans and venting dryers cut moisture at the source. Damp that keeps coming back may be a leak or rising damp, which airing can’t fix.
